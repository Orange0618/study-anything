# Evidence and acceptance guidelines

Evidence should demonstrate the target capability, not merely activity.

## Evidence quality ladder

From weakest to strongest:

1. A resource was opened or a course was watched.
2. Notes were copied or summarized.
3. A provided example was reproduced.
4. An input, parameter, assumption, or scenario was changed and explained.
5. The learner completed a new task independently and passed an objective check or explicit rubric.
6. Multiple capabilities were integrated, limitations were analyzed, and the result was reproducible by another person.

Use the lowest level that genuinely proves the current goal, but do not confuse levels 1 or 2 with mastery.

## Acceptance criteria

A useful criterion is:

- observable;
- specific to the capability;
- proportionate to the learning stage;
- checkable from saved evidence;
- repeatable under stated conditions.

Examples:

- “Explain attention” is vague.
- “Given a three-token example, calculate attention weights, verify the implementation output, and explain the mask behavior” is checkable.

- “Learn user interviews” is vague.
- “Write an interview guide, conduct two interviews, code the notes, and identify three findings with supporting quotes” is checkable.

## Completion rule

A task may be marked complete only when:

1. its acceptance criterion has been applied;
2. evidence exists at the referenced local path or stable external location;
3. a review records the result, problems, conclusion, and next action;
4. the progress table links both evidence and review.

If acceptance fails, keep the task in progress or blocked and create a review explaining the failure and recovery plan.
