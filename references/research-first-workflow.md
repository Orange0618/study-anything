# Research-first workflow

Use this reference when the requested field is unfamiliar, broad, ambiguous, or likely to have changed.

## Purpose

Research is not a decorative reading list. It gives the learner enough context to make an informed decision about scope and depth.

## Research questions

Answer the smallest useful subset:

1. What does the field enable people to understand or do?
2. What knowledge and tools are genuinely foundational?
3. Which dependencies constrain the order of practice?
4. What are representative tasks, projects, assessments, or professional outcomes?
5. What does competence look like at two or three plausible depths?
6. Which topics are optional, specialized, obsolete, or frequently overemphasized?
7. What environment, equipment, data, budget, or access constraints matter?
8. Which claims require current verification?

## Source selection

Prefer, in order:

1. standards, official documentation, primary research, or first-party curricula;
2. respected textbooks, institutions, or maintainers;
3. high-quality practitioner material for implementation details;
4. community material only when it adds real-world failure modes or comparisons.

Record the title, link or local path, access date, authority type, relevant topic, and how the source influenced the workbench. Do not collect sources without explaining their role.

## Depth options

Translate research into two or three concrete options. Each option should state:

- observable capabilities;
- expected practice;
- final evidence;
- typical effort or prerequisites;
- what remains outside scope.

Avoid asking the learner to choose between labels that have not been explained.

## Research output

Write `调研/领域调研.md` with:

- field overview;
- knowledge structure and dependencies;
- practical applications;
- depth options;
- recommended option and reasoning;
- risks and deferred topics;
- open questions for the learner.

Write `调研/资料来源.md` as the source ledger.
